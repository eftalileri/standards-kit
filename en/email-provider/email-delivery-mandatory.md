## **EDP-M** — Mandatory

| ID | Rule | Consequence | Verify |
|----|------|-------------|--------|
| **EDP-M1** | Durable delivery for state-coupled email. When GEDP1 selects a durable queue or outbox, persist the send intent atomically with the state change where the datastore supports that guarantee; invoke the provider only after commit through a worker. Select a durability mechanism compatible with multi-instance operation and the system's failure model. | Inline-send ambiguity can desynchronize state and email — bug. | The state change and durable intent share the required atomic boundary; provider calls run after commit. |
| **EDP-M2** | Idempotent send keyed by `messageId`. Every delivery is idempotent on the app-generated `messageId` (**EMP-M11**) so a worker retry or a user retry never double-sends. The `messageId` is the dedupe key (reusing [idempotency](../idempotency/idempotency.md) discipline); a row already marked delivered is never re-dispatched. | Duplicate emails on retry — bug ([transactions **TX-REC-2**](../transactions/transaction-policy.md): atomicity does not imply idempotent retries). | One delivered state per `messageId`; replayed drain does not re-send. |
| **EDP-M3** | Worker coordination and observation. Coordinate workers across instances so concurrent drains do not create duplicate sends. Monitor worker liveness and surface stalled or failed processing through the application's observability system. | Duplicate processing or a silently stalled queue — bug. | A cross-instance coordination mechanism and worker health signal are documented and observable. |
| **EDP-M4** | Bounded retry → backoff → terminal failure. Retry transient failures with an exponential or otherwise documented backoff and a finite cap. Do not retry permanent failures automatically. Route failures through the application's established error and metric channels by cause, record enough context for diagnosis, and retain failed records only for a documented review period. Choose retry and retention limits for the product's provider and operational needs. | Unbounded retry or silent delivery loss — bug. | A finite retry policy, explicit terminal state, cause-appropriate observability, and bounded retention are documented. |
| **EDP-M5** | Recipient-side deliverability is observed. Track delivery, bounce, and complaint signals where the provider exposes them; configure alarm thresholds from current provider guidance and the product's risk tolerance. Reconcile feedback to the original send using a stable application correlation identifier and update the suppression authority for qualifying events (**EDP-M9**). | Reputation decay can degrade delivery — bug. | Metrics and alarms are documented; feedback is correlated to a send and qualifying events update suppression. |
| **EDP-M6** | Provider and region selection are explicit. Document the active provider and region policy. Any multi-region or cross-provider failover must be an intentional, observable design with clear retry and idempotency semantics; do not silently fail over. | Hidden failover creates an opaque failure mode — bug. | Configuration and any failover behavior are explicit and monitored. |
| **EDP-M7** | Render once at enqueue where message consistency requires it. The outbox row stores the rendered content produced by the template authority (**EMP-M10**); the worker does not re-render it. Treat queued content as sensitive: do not log it, restrict access, and remove delivered content promptly. Where a credential appears in the body, its validity window and outbox-retention policy must be designed together with the credential authority (**EMP-M12**). | Queued and sent content drift, or sensitive content is retained too long — bug. | The worker sends the stored body without re-rendering; access and retention are documented. |
| **EDP-M8** | Retention has one authority. A single documented policy owns outbox and audit retention; all expiry and purge mechanisms consume it. Remove delivered content promptly where possible and retain failed records only for a bounded, justified review period. | Unbounded growth or conflicting retention periods — bug. | One policy is consumed by expiry and purge paths; retained records have a bounded lifetime. |
| **EDP-M9** | Suppression has one authority. A single app-owned suppression store is the sole source of "do not email this address." Every send checks it first; bounce / complaint reconciliation (**EDP-M5**), an admin action, and any provider-side suppression (e.g. SES account-level) all reconcile into this one store — the provider list is a secondary safety net, never a competing authority. On conflict, the app store is the answer. | Three rival suppression sources (SES vs app vs admin) that disagree — bug (parallel SSoT, **SSG-M8**). | One suppression store; send path consults it; provider/admin/bounce all write the same store. |
| **EDP-M10** | Provider operational health is observed (distinct from deliverability). Beyond recipient-side rates (**EDP-M5**), emit operational metrics — send success rate, send latency, provider error rate — and alarm on a provider-error spike / latency blow-out (a provider outage shows here long before bounce rates move). A provider-error surge ties to the dead-letter alarm (**EDP-M4**). The outbox's oldest pending age and dead-letter count are sampled every worker run and alarmed on absolute thresholds — a queue stuck past its retry horizon is the outage the user sees. These are derived observations of send outcomes, not a new authority. | A silent provider outage (mail piling up, no alarm) — bug. | Success-rate / latency / error-rate metrics exist with alarms; a provider-error spike is visible. |

**EDP-M9** unfed-authority application: A suppression authority is a control, not a container, and it is only in force while something writes to it — so the gate and its producer are one deliverable, and shipping the gate alone is not a partial delivery but an absent one. The pre-send read can be correct, type-safe, fail-closed and covered by a lint lock while suppressing nothing at all, because the missing half is a producer that was never built, and code that does not exist leaves no trace for a reviewer, a linter or a dead-code scan to catch — every check is green precisely because there is nothing to check. This is the deliverability failure at its most expensive: mail keeps going to addresses the provider has already rejected, and the reputation damage lands on every other message the account sends, including those of users who have nothing to do with the offending address. Three obligations follow. (a) At least one producer is automatic. A store that only an operator can fill by hand is unfed in practice; the **EDP-M5** reconciliation (or an equivalent provider feedback sink) is what discharges this rule, and an admin action is an addition to it, never a substitute. (b) Until the producer exists, the gap is declared where the gate is read. The absence is written at the consulting site — not left for the next reader to infer from a passing check — together with the measured store count, so that "the gate is in place" cannot be mistaken for "addresses are being suppressed". (c) An empty store is reported as unfed, not as clean. A zero count means "nothing writes here" until a producer is shown to exist; reading it as "no address needs suppressing" inverts the evidence and converts a live defect into a reassurance. [On violation: Bug — bounced and complained-about addresses are never suppressed, the sender reputation of every user's mail decays, and the defect is invisible from inside the repository] [Verify by: a named automatic producer bound to the single store; the store's document count measured against the live corpus rather than assumed; where no producer exists yet, the declaration present at the read site with that count. *Stated enforcement limit:* no source-tree scan can distinguish "this authority has no caller because its feeder is unbuilt" from "this authority is dead code" — both are the same absence of references, and the whole-graph reporter that would surface it is advisory by policy ([**DCD-M1**](../dead-code/dead-code.md)), so this row is review-bound at the point the gate is introduced.]

**EDP-M9** permanence application: Whether a suppression is permanent **MUST** have exactly one answer, and the permanent/transient axis belongs in the store's write contract rather than in a stored field. A transient bounce (a full mailbox, a temporary defer) is not a suppression event at all under **EDP-M5**, yet the reason vocabulary alone cannot refuse it — a caller that maps a transient provider event onto the permanent reason writes a row that looks identical to a genuine one, and the address is then never mailed again on the strength of a condition that has since cleared. Storing the axis as its own field does not fix this and adds a defect of its own: the reason already determines permanence, so a second field is a derived value persisted as an authority ([**SSG-M8**](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)) and the two can diverge with no error surface. Put the axis in the type of the write: the writer accepts an event whose bounce branch admits only the permanent classification, so a caller holding a raw provider classification cannot construct the call without narrowing first, and the compiler refuses the mistake instead of every caller having to remember the rule. Derive the branches from the reason set rather than restating it, so a reason added later inherits the contract instead of silently escaping it. [On violation: Bug — a temporarily undeliverable address is suppressed for good, or two stored fields disagree about whether a suppression is permanent] [Verify by: the transient classification unrepresentable in the writer's parameter type; no stored permanence field alongside the reason; the event branches derived from the reason set's single authority]

**EDP-M5** unclassifiable-feedback application: A feedback sink translates a vocabulary it does not own, so a member of that vocabulary it cannot recognise **MUST** yield no write *and* a signal on a watched channel — while still being acknowledged as delivered. Two distinct failures hide in that one branch. First, refusing to act is *silent by construction*: declining to suppress an unrecognised provider classification is the correct action, but it leaves no row, no error and nothing that separates it from a sink that was never wired — so a class the provider introduced after the mapping was written quietly stops reaching the authority while the store's count still reads healthy. That is the unfed-authority defect arriving one class at a time rather than all at once, and it is harder to see precisely because the rest of the sink works. Second, the reflex remedy — answering the unrecognised event with a failure status so that it is "not lost" — is worse: a provider that retries on failure will redeliver a message the sink can never process for the whole life of its retry policy, converting one vocabulary gap into sustained load carrying no new information. The two questions are on different axes and must be answered separately: the wire status reports whether the message was *received and settled*, the observability channel reports whether it was *understood*. So acknowledge success, write nothing, and raise the unrecognised member on the alert channel, while the members the mapping deliberately excludes stay on the metric channel — a refusal the rule anticipated is not an incident; a vocabulary the rule has never seen is. [On violation: Bug — a provider classification added after the mapping was written silently stops feeding the authority, or the sink answers an unprocessable message with a failure status and the provider redelivers it for the life of its retry policy] [Verify by: the classifier total over the provider vocabulary with an explicit refusal branch; unrecognised members distinguished from anticipated refusals in both the returned result type and the log channel; a success wire status on every refusal branch]
