## Zero trust — membership gates, decision inputs and recovery anchors

> The identifier half of this band lives next door: the tenant proof required before a caller-supplied identifier reaches a globally keyed identity collection, and what a cross-subject surface may carry, are [SEC-M-ZT-4](security-global-identity-lookup.md).

SEC-M-ZT-5: A tenant/organization identifier supplied by the caller — HTTP query, body, path param, or an optional "active context" hint carried in an options object — **MUST** pass a single membership-validation authority before it is used to derive any authorization value (actor/principal object, role, access level, capability flag, or permission-bearing session). Two properties bind. (a) One gate, one answer: the question "is this subject a member of this tenant?" has exactly one authority; a second gate on the same path is forbidden even when it looks like defense in depth, because two gates drift and the *earlier* one silently wins — including pre-empting any reconciliation the real authority performs. Put the gate in the authority that derives the value, not in each caller: a transport-layer handler forwarding raw input into a validating domain authority is correct layering, whereas an unvalidated identifier reaching the derivation is the defect. (b) Absence of membership is not a level: when no membership record exists, the access level, role, or tier **MUST NOT** be defaulted, downgraded, or otherwise invented — that silently mints a valid-looking principal for a subject whose membership was revoked or never existed. Fail loud with an explicit, registered "denied for this tenant" outcome (expected/business severity, not a crash-report). A subject holding a *different* relationship to that tenant (e.g. a subject-class record) does not thereby become staff there: a role label stored globally on the subject is not evidence of membership in the tenant currently being acted on — membership is per-tenant and **must** be read per-tenant. Where a revoked-membership subject needs lifecycle reconciliation (demotion, session downgrade), that reconciliation belongs behind the same single gate so denial and self-healing cannot diverge. (c) Relaxing a global-label gate is NOT divisible. When a product decision converts a mutually-exclusive global role label into per-tenant membership, every gate keying on that label is one change: the enrolment/join gate, the write-path gate that repeats it, and the actor derivation that will have to serve the newly admitted subject. Landing a subset is worse than either end state — the subject passes the relaxed entrance and is then refused by an unrelaxed one, so the capability reads as granted and fails only at use, and it fails as a *registered denial that looks correct*, which is why review accepts it. The order is fixed: make the per-tenant derivation authoritative first, so every entrance that is later opened already has a working destination; only then remove the label gates — never the reverse. Enumerate the set by the label, not by the feature: a label written by more than one flow is read by more gates than the feature's own surface suggests, and the ones outside that surface are exactly the ones a scoped change misses. That first step has exactly one safe shape: the derivation DEMOTES, it never promotes. Where the global label finds no match in the target tenant, the subject may be reduced to the relationship it *can* prove there and to nothing else — the proof demanded is the same proof that relationship already demands of everyone, and the principal produced **must** be indistinguishable from the one that relationship already mints, which is what makes the step's safety a finite enumeration instead of a promise: every consumer downstream already has to reject that weaker class, so no surface becomes reachable. The reverse direction — letting a weaker label claim the stronger per-tenant relationship it happens to hold a record for — is a privilege *elevation*, belongs to no step of this sequence unless the product decision names it, and is the shape that re-opens this row: widening the role model and widening the tenant boundary are two different changes, and landing them as one is how a relaxation turns into a leak. A non-throwing sibling introduced to let the derivation *ask* rather than *fail* carries (b)'s obligation unchanged — its "no record" answer is still not a level, and now it is a value that flows instead of an exception that stops. A RESOLVED tenant scope is not an AUTHORIZED one, and a tenant-scoped SIDE EFFECT takes the authorized value. This row is usually read as binding authorization values only, and that reading leaves a live hole: a request-scoped resolver that answers *which tenant is this request aiming at* — applying precedence, normalisation and canonicalisation — has still not asked *may this actor reach that tenant*. Any consumer producing a durable or observable effect attributed to a tenant is in scope even when it grants nothing and leaks nothing: advancing a read-cache generation, keying an idempotency scope, moving a counter, enqueuing work, stamping a tenant field into a log. An authenticated actor who can name another tenant in the request body can otherwise make that tenant's caches, counters and queues move — noise and cost, not disclosure, which is exactly why it survives review. Three obligations follow. (1) Carry the two answers in separate, separately named slots. "Scope requested" and "scope proven" are different data; one field serving both is the defect, because every later reader inherits the meaning the field's *name* suggests rather than the one it holds — and a name that overstates ("verified scope" on a resolver's output) manufactures that misreading. The proven slot has exactly one writer: the membership authority's success path. (2) A consumer that cannot throw still reads the proven slot. Post-response hooks and background seams often cannot call the validating resolver at all, because it raises where nothing can catch it — that is what pushes authors back to the raw surface. The answer is a non-throwing read of the already-proven value; where nothing was proven this request, the consumer does nothing, and an absent proof is never a licence to fall back to the requested value. (3) Prove the switch loses no coverage before making it. Reading the proven slot fires on strictly fewer requests than reading the resolved one, so enumerate the surfaces the effect **must** still cover and show each one passes the membership authority before its success response; a surface that does not is not coverage the switch removed — it is a surface that was moving another tenant's state all along. [Consequence: Critical security bug — privilege retention after removal / cross-tenant actor minting; **must not** ship] [Verify: For every authorization-derivation call, trace its tenant argument to either a validated authority result or a non-caller-controlled source; assert the membership lookup has no "record missing → default level" branch. Single-file AST rules CAN express the common case — flag a caller-supplied tenant id reaching the actor-derivation call in a file that never invokes the membership authority.]

SEC-M-ZT-8: Where an actor resolved AGAINST THE TENANT is available, the subject's RAW GLOBAL role label **MUST NOT** be an input to the same authorization decision — and the closure belongs at the TYPE level, not only at the lint level. SEC-M-ZT-5(b) forbids reading a globally stored role label as evidence of membership; this row binds the shape that keeps re-opening it — a derivation that *does* receive a tenant-resolved actor and *also* accepts a role/level/flag beside it, then branches on whichever is convenient. The same human is legitimately privileged in one tenant and a mere end user in another, so the global label answers a question the decision never asked; where that label is the branch taken, the tenant-aware reduction is never reached and the gate it feeds is never consulted — no denial is logged, because no gate ran. Three obligations bind.
- (a) The signature is the fence. The authorization derivation's parameter list takes the resolved actor and nothing that can stand in for it: no role string, no access-level string, no admin flag lifted off the subject record. What a function cannot receive, it cannot decide on — and this is why the fix is a type change, not a comparison ban.
- (b) A second tenant identifier beside the actor goes with it. Once the actor carries the tenant it was resolved for, a sibling tenant-id parameter is a second answer to *which tenant* ([SSG-M8](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)) and lets a caller pair one tenant's actor with another tenant's scope. Where the actor's tenant **must** also satisfy a branded repository key, the brand rides on a server-only actor view minted with the scope ([REP-N-4b](../repository/repository-contract.md)(g)), never on a sibling parameter. The same reduction applies to the deny-by-default policy itself: a copy of it beside the authority is a second policy, and the two drift.
- (c) Prefer the type gate over the lint gate, and STATE which half each covers. A parameter that no longer exists is enforced by the compiler across the whole repository, with no governed file list to maintain and no exemption set to grow ([FP-M18](../false-positive/check-design-rules.md)); a lint rule covers only the files it was pointed at and only the shapes it recognises. Where both are possible the type change is the fence, and the check takes the remainder the type system cannot express — a raw label already in hand being compared against a privileged constant. Naming that division is part of the fence: an unstated remainder reads as covered ([KIT-INV-8](../authoring/kit-structural-invariants.md)).

[Consequence: Critical security bug — a privilege decision taken from a label that belongs to a different tenant, with the tenant-aware gate never reached; **must not** ship] [Verify: read the authorization derivation's signature and confirm it takes the resolved actor and carries no role/level/flag parameter and no second tenant identifier; then confirm the deny-by-default reduction has exactly one definition. Single-file AST rules CAN express the comparison half — a raw subject role label tested against a privileged constant in a file that also holds a resolved actor.]

SEC-M-ZT-3: When recovery, ownership reconciliation, or safe retry of a downstream provider side effect depends on first persisting a verified reusable provider reference, that reference write is a required prerequisite, not fail-soft enrichment. The verified reference and provenance **MUST** be durably committed before any dependent referral/reward, lifecycle, access, or external mutation side effect. Reference persistence failure aborts the current attempt and remains retryable from the durable event/inbox; it **MUST NOT** continue without the recovery anchor or fall through to an alternate side-effect method. [Consequence: Critical security/integrity bug — an external effect can succeed without a durable ownership/recovery trail, making safe replay impossible.] [Verify: Control-flow ordering from runtime-validated provider evidence → required reference write → dependent side effect; forced persistence failure produces no downstream effect.]
