## Zero trust — consented publication of a tenant profile

SEC-M-ZT-7: A cross-subject surface MAY carry per-subject attributes in exactly one case — a tenant-published profile, where the subject tenant itself decided that those attributes are public. This is the single bounded exception to the cross-subject clause of [SEC-M-ZT-4](security-tenant-scope-gates.md), and what bounds it is that the distinguishing fact belongs to the subject, not to the consumer: the rule protects subjects who never consented, and a subject that recorded its own publication decision is not that subject. Five conditions bind together. They are not a menu: if any one of them is missing, [SEC-M-ZT-4](security-tenant-scope-gates.md) applies unchanged and the surface may publish no per-subject attribute at all — four out of five is an ordinary cross-subject surface wearing a consent label, which is the more dangerous state of the two, because the label stops the next reviewer from asking.
- (a) The consent is recorded, revocable, and owned by the subject. Publication rests on an explicit opt-in that the subject tenant recorded for itself: held by that tenant's own authority, written only through that tenant's own write path, and revocable through the same path. Consent inferred from a different act is not consent — not a signup, not a purchase, not a completed field, and not the mere existence of the record; "the row is there, so it may be shown" is precisely the reasoning [SEC-M-ZT-4](security-tenant-scope-gates.md) forbids. A flag an operator can set on the tenant's behalf is not consent either, because the subject is then not the writer. Absence of a recorded opt-in is a denial, never a default — the same fail-loud shape [SEC-M-ZT-5](security-tenant-scope-gates.md)(b) requires when a membership record is missing.
- (b) The published field set is a closed allowlist, and the response is built by inclusion. The set is declared — enumerated in the consuming project's own authority registry, where a reviewer can read it without reading the query — and each response is assembled by naming every field that travels. Building it by taking the stored record and removing what **must not** travel is forbidden: an exclusion list is correct only until the next field is added to the record, it fails open, and nothing about that failure is visible at the call site. Widening what is published is therefore a change to the declaration, reviewed as one; it is never a side effect of a schema edit.
- (c) Nothing internal, and nothing about a natural person. No entry in the allowlist may be an internal identifier, a credential or capability, a commercial state (subscription, tier, balance, arrears, counts of the tenant's own subjects), or an attribute of a natural person rather than of the business. This is the line the consent cannot cross: a tenant may publish facts about itself as a business, and it cannot consent on behalf of the people inside or around it. A contact point is publishable only where it is the business's own published contact — never an account identity, because publishing an account identity publishes a login handle, and where that handle is the account's key it publishes an enumerable one, which is the failure [SEC-M-ZT-4](security-tenant-scope-gates.md)'s pseudonym paragraph already measures.
- (d) The surface carries anti-enumeration controls, and all three are required. Consent to be listed is not consent to be harvested. The surface authenticates its caller; it rate-limits on an identity the caller cannot rotate (a bucket key drawn from a supplied value — an address, a device id, a client-chosen token — resets the counter and is not a limit, [SEC-M-RL-3](security-rate-limiting.md)); and it bounds page size at the server, so a caller-supplied size cannot lift it. With any of the three missing, the allowlisted set is downloadable in bulk and the enumeration oracle returns — assembled one lawful row at a time, which is why counting rows individually never detects it.
- (e) Withdrawal takes effect without waiting for a scheduled process. Revoking the opt-in removes the subject from the surface on the next read. Where the read filters a derived publication flag rather than the consent field itself, that flag has exactly one writer and that writer recomputes it on the revocation event ([SSG-M17](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md), [SSG-M8](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)); a periodic sweep is a backstop for missed events and never the mechanism. A withdrawal whose effect waits for a job is a subject that is still published after saying stop, and the interval is the exact size of the breach.

[Consequence: Critical security bug — cross-tenant PII disclosure and enumeration oracle published under a consent label; **must not** ship] [Verify: the five conditions by review, their failure shapes by the locks below. For each such surface open, in order: (1) the consent authority, and confirm the subject tenant is its only writer and that no read defaults a missing record to published; (2) the projection, and confirm every field it emits is named in the declared set and that the set is built by inclusion; (3) the declared set itself, and confirm no entry is an internal identifier, a credential, a commercial state or a natural-person attribute; (4) the route wiring, and confirm authentication, an identity-keyed rate limit whose key the caller cannot rotate, and a server-bounded page size; (5) the path from revocation to the read filter, and confirm no scheduled job stands between them.]

Check reach: none of the five conditions is decidable by a single-file check — the evidence is a data flow between artefacts (the consent field's writer set, the projection against a declared allowlist in another language, a semantic classification of each field, the mounted middleware chain, the absence of a scheduler between revocation and read) — so the conditions themselves are held by review.

What IS mechanically decidable — the SHAPE of the closure, not its content. Every failure shape below compiles, produces no runtime signal, and is exactly what a later edit introduces. Four predicates live entirely in one syntax tree and **MUST** be locked wherever the surface exists. (1) The projection's form. An exclusion entry (a field mapped to the "omit" value) anywhere in the projection stage is forbidden, with the single exemption of the driver-supplied default key, whose omission *narrows* the set rather than widening it; a projection assembled into a variable is the same object and its later writes count. Rationale for (b): an exclusion list is correct only until the next schema field is added, and it fails open. (2) The wire-row builder's form. A plain spread of the source row into the returned object is forbidden — it re-opens (1) from behind and passes whatever the projection later leaks. A conditional spread whose operand is a literal object (`...(x !== undefined && { x })`) is legitimate and **MUST** be distinguished, because it can only carry the names written beside it; failing to separate the two is what makes naive versions of this check unusable. (3) The row contract stays closed. The schema that validates the returned row **must** retain its "reject unknown keys" modifier; without it a field that leaked past (1) is silently swallowed instead of raising, and the violation becomes invisible. (4) The publication gate is a constant. Where the list query filters on a derived publication flag, that filter's value is the published-state literal. A type that merely requires the *field* to be present accepts the opposite literal, it compiles, and every unpublished — that is, non-consenting — subject enters the public list in one character. Each of the four is field- and file-scoped by construction, so a new surface does not inherit the lock and needs its own. What a project can additionally fence, and should, is that the surface's response type is the declared allowlist type and no wider one, which a type system decides inside one compilation unit.

SEC-M-ZT-7 note (single-subject public surface): A surface that returns one subject's attributes and is reached only through that subject's own published key (a tenant-chosen slug or link) is not a cross-subject surface, so (d)'s authenticated caller does not apply; it MAY be unauthenticated while every bound below holds, and with any one missing it is held to SEC-M-ZT-7 in full. (1) Conditions (a), (b), (c) and (e) bind unchanged. The one admissible natural-person entry is the minimal display label (given name, surname initial) of a person the tenant itself offers as a selectable resource on that surface — never a contact point or account identity — and it leaves the surface on the next read when the tenant withdraws that person. (2) No endpoint lists, searches or resolves subjects other than by the exact key, and an unknown, withdrawn or not-entitled key answers one identical response, so the surface is no oracle for which keys exist. (3) The key is a pointer, never authority: every write the surface triggers re-resolves the subject from the key on the server and re-applies its current consent and entitlement in the same request. (4) Volumetric limits key on the source and on the target subject, and any cost the surface spends on the subject's behalf is a per-subject ceiling that fails closed ([SEC-M-RL-3](security-rate-limiting.md)(i)). Reason: the key is public by design — without these bounds the page is an unauthenticated cross-subject surface one key at a time, and the subject pays for its abuse. [Consequence: Critical security bug — key oracle, natural-person disclosure or tenant-paid abuse through an unauthenticated page] [Verify: review per surface — the route set has no list/search route, every not-found branch returns one shape, the projection matches the declared allowlist, every write re-resolves from the key, limiter keys and the per-subject ceiling's fail-closed branch.]
