# Scheduled work — expiring eligibility and the undelivered terminal (**SJO-M8**)

```kit-cover
type: normative
part-of: README.md
binds: the terminal outcome a recurring run owes when its candidates lose eligibility permanently, and the retention boundary that makes such a delivery unauditable
open-when: shape:backend-heavy, signal:scheduled-job, signal:background-worker, signal:notification-dispatch, signal:notification-lifecycle, signal:delivery-status
skip-when: signal:client-only

```

Scope: **SJO-M8** governs the output of work whose delivery eligibility window closes permanently.

---

## **MUST** (normative) — **SJO-M8**

**SJO-M8**: Where a candidate's eligibility EXPIRES — it leaves the job's candidate set permanently and a missed turn is never retried — "nobody was due" and "nobody due could be reached" **MUST** be two DIFFERENT terminal outcomes, and the job **MUST** declare that it is in this class. [**SJO-M3**](scheduled-work-liveness-and-outcome.md)'s class (a) is still the honest class for such a job: an hour with no due candidate really is ordinary. But the family's existing vocabulary then offers only `empty`, and `empty` is what both facts produce, because both end the run with an output magnitude of zero. The two facts are not comparable: one is the system working, the other is a product obligation that has just been lost and cannot be re-attempted. Four obligations. (a) The job declares expiring eligibility in the same declaration that carries its cadence and outcome class. Whether a missed turn is recoverable is a product statement about the job's candidate predicate — does the row fall out of that query for good? — and nothing in the source derives it; a job that does not declare it is asserting that its backlog waits. (b) The run records the distinct terminal when at least one candidate reached the delivery step and none of them was delivered, and it records it on the same durable trace [**SJO-M2**](scheduled-work-liveness-and-outcome.md) already keeps — never in a log line alone, because a log line is not queryable by the supervisor, which is the whole point of **SJO-M3**. The magnitude of the miss belongs on that record for the same reason, in its own field: folding it into the output count would make one number mean both "three were delivered" and "three were lost". (c) A skip that is not a loss is NOT counted. A candidate outside its delivery window, cancelled by a later event, or already served is not a missed delivery, and folding those into the distinct terminal turns it into noise that fires every ordinary hour — which retires the signal exactly as alert flooding does ([**SJO-M4**](scheduled-work-liveness-and-outcome.md)). The counted population is the candidates that reached the side effect. (d) The distinct terminal is VISIBLE, not an alarm. An unreachable recipient is an expected/business outcome and takes the expected-failure level and the metric channel ([**M16**](failure-level-and-channel-routing.md), [**M17**](failure-level-and-channel-routing.md)); the supervisor reports it beside its other non-breach states and **MUST NOT** open an episode for it. A run that completed is a completion: it closes a liveness episode exactly as a healthy run does, and a surface that renders health **MUST NOT** read this terminal as "the job has stopped" — that sentence asserts the one thing that did not happen, which is **SJO-M4**'s false-recovery defect seen from the other side. [Consequence: The most expensive one-shot failure class — the run is green, the counter is zero, and a delivery whose window has closed forever is indistinguishable from an idle hour.] [Verify: the declaration carries the class + a run with one undeliverable due candidate writes the distinct terminal and its magnitude to the durable trace + a run with no candidates still writes the ordinary empty terminal + the supervisor reports the distinct terminal without opening an episode + the health surface treats it as a completion.]

Two sibling jobs **MUST NOT** answer "is this recipient reachable?" two ways. Where one delivery path pre-checks permission before the dispatcher and another lets the dispatcher terminalise the attempt, the same unreachable recipient leaves a durable record on one path and nothing on the other — one fact, two answers ([**SSG-M8**](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)). Reducing to one answer is required, but which answer is not free: letting the dispatcher persist the attempt is only equivalent where the delivery's idempotency key is scoped to a repeating period. Where the key is time-independent — one notice per subject, forever — a terminal non-retryable failure row consumes that key permanently, so a recipient who becomes reachable the next day is never served. Choosing that shape for such a key trades a delivery guarantee for a record and is a regression, not a simplification. The durable answer then belongs on a record whose lifetime matches the obligation (below), and the pre-check stays.

Retention is an audit boundary, and it is silent. Where the record that would answer "was this obligation discharged?" lives in a store sized for the *user's* inbox — a message log under a short retention window — the answer evaporates on that window's schedule, and a later count over that store cannot answer a question about an earlier period in either direction. This is the mirror of [**SJO-N2**](scheduled-work-prohibitions-and-enforcement.md): absence of failure records is not evidence of liveness, and absence of success records under a retention window is not evidence of non-delivery. A project measuring such a question states the retention window beside the count, or the measurement is not a measurement. Where the obligation outlives the message log, the durable run trace of **SJO-M2** — or the record that already knows the obligation was *created* — carries the discharge fact, because its lifetime is at least the lifetime of the obligation.

A uniqueness constraint inherits the retention of the store it is declared on. A one-shot notice is often defended by a database unique index over a time-independent key — one notice per subject, forever. That defence is real only while the row it collides with exists. Declare it on a store under a retention window and the key is *released* when the row expires, so the guarantee's true duration is the retention window, not "forever", and a comment naming that index as the uniqueness authority becomes false on a schedule nothing announces. The comparison that decides whether this bites is between two windows a project can read off its own source: how long a row stays in the job's candidate set, against how long the evidence of the discharge survives. Where candidacy can outlast the evidence, the notice repeats — and the excess is the ordinary case rather than an edge, because the candidate window is usually a product horizon (an entitlement's remaining term, a grace period) built from settings a later operator may raise, while the retention window is an unrelated storage decision. Both numbers are written next to each other where the delivery is decided, and the discharge fact is moved to a record with no retention index — which, once that field is named, is the one part of this a single-file schema check *can* decide: the home of a permanent fact must not declare an expiry. [Consequence: A gate that reads "never sent" from an expired row sends a second, irreversible message to the same person.]

Check reach. A single-file AST check decides the consistency of (a) and (b): the declaration object and the runner body sit in one file, so a rule can require that a job declaring expiring eligibility reaches the distinct-terminal call, and the reverse — that a job recording the distinct terminal declares the class. Making that check bidirectional is what stops the declaration from becoming a flag nothing reads, which is the failure mode [**SJO-M7**](scheduled-work-boot-arming.md)(e) names. The check cannot decide the root question — *which* jobs have expiring eligibility, or more generally which persisted state change obliges a user-facing notice that a later run cannot make good. Three declarations no repository has are what such a check would need: (1) a machine-readable statement that a given persisted state transition obliges a notice; (2) a machine-readable statement of which persisted field discharges that obligation; (3) the expiring-eligibility statement of (a), which is precisely why (a) is a declaration rather than a derivation. Nor can any single-file check see the historical shape of this defect — one job writing the field another job selects the absence of, ordered by two schedules that resolve in different frames — because that is semantics over query intent across files. A project therefore fences the jobs whose eligibility it has established and records the remainder as unclassified; reading the absence of findings as coverage is the inversion **SJO-N2** names.
