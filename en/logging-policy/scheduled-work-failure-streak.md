# Scheduled work — partial failure and the consecutive-failure counter (**SJO-M9**)


**Keywords:** scheduled job, cron, partial failure, failure rate, consecutive failures, flapping job, supervisor sampling, liveness blind spot

Scope: **SJO-M9** governs durable consecutive-failure evidence and tolerance thresholds; **SJO-M12** governs runs that overrun their cadence. Failure classification follows the error boundary; retry/backoff belongs to the job.

---

## **MUST** (normative) — **SJO-M9**

**SJO-M9**: A liveness predicate can only see SILENCE, so a recurring unit of work **MUST** also carry a **CONSECUTIVE-FAILURE** counter on the same durable record, with a threshold it declares. The durable trace of [**SJO-M2**](scheduled-work-liveness-and-outcome.md) is written on success; a run that throws leaves nothing behind. The supervisor's late predicate therefore asks one question — *did anything succeed within cadence + grace?* — and a single success between two failures answers "yes" and resets the clock. A job that fails most of its ticks but succeeds occasionally is, to that predicate, almost always healthy: the rate of partial failure is measured by no channel at all. Six obligations.

(a) The threshold is DECLARED, beside the cadence and grace. How many consecutive rejections a job may absorb is a product statement about that job — a dependency that flaps tolerates a short run; a job that fires twice a day has already lost a day by its second failure — and nothing in the source derives it. It lives in the [**SJO-M1**](scheduled-work-liveness-and-outcome.md) declaration so a newly added job cannot inherit a number nobody chose for it. The threshold **MUST** be at least 2: a threshold of 1 raises per failed run, which is exactly the per-evaluation alerting [**SJO-M4**](scheduled-work-liveness-and-outcome.md) forbids, and the declaration refuses it where it is written rather than accepting it and flooding later.

(b) The counter is written by the SAME shared seam as the completion trace, onto the SAME record. A failing run is, by definition, the run least able to write anything; a counter maintained inside each job body would be exactly as reliable as the job whose reliability is the question — **SJO-M2**'s reasoning applied to the mirror fact. A separate "failure count" collection beside the record that already knows when the job last succeeded is a second writer of one job's state and the two will disagree ([**SSG-M8**](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)). The counted population is the runs that held the work's exclusion claim and produced no durable completion; a tick that never acquired the claim did not run, and its own silence is what the liveness fence already measures.

(c) The reset is part of the completion write, never a second write. An ordinary success clears the streak in the same update that records the completion. Split into two writes, a process that dies between them leaves a job that succeeded looking like a job that is failing — and the repair for that is a second reconciliation path, which is the defect compounding.

(d) The counter alone is NOT readable by a sampling supervisor — the record **MUST** also carry the PEAK since the supervisor last consumed it. The supervisor of **SJO-M4** evaluates on its own cadence, and a success resets the streak: a run of failures that opens and closes between two evaluations leaves no trace at all in a current-value counter. The peak is raised in the same atomic write that increments the streak, and the judgement reads the greater of the two. Consuming it is mandatory and belongs to the supervisor, in the same write that opens or continues the episode: an unconsumed peak keeps the breach true forever, so the job would recover and the episode would never close — **SJO-M4**'s false-recovery prohibition seen from the other side, where the alert that never clears retires itself just as surely as one that clears wrongly. Consuming the peak while the current streak still stands is not a tolerance widening ([**SJO-N3**](scheduled-work-prohibitions-and-enforcement.md)): the breach continues to hold on the live counter.

(e) The alert carries the MEASURED run length and the threshold in force. The threshold is a chosen number, not a verdict, and the only way to move it with evidence rather than opinion is for each alert to state what was actually observed against what was demanded. A project that carries only "threshold exceeded" has to re-derive the distribution from logs to tune the number, which is the point at which nobody tunes it.

(f) A counter that is present but unreadable is NOT zero. Any comparison against a non-numeric or corrupt value is false, so a broken counter would report "no breach" forever — the inversion [**SJO-N2**](scheduled-work-prohibitions-and-enforcement.md) names, arriving through arithmetic instead of through an empty query. Such a row is reported as unmeasurable and keeps any open episode, exactly as an unreadable declaration does. An absent field is a different fact and is legitimately zero: nothing has failed yet, and the positive completion trace is what carries the health claim.

[Consequence: The whole liveness apparatus reports green while a job discards most of its work. This is strictly worse than no monitoring, because the green is read as a measurement; and the class survives every later "severity reduction" of the underlying error — the moment a noisy provider failure is correctly demoted out of the crash channel, a system without this counter has replaced its only accidental signal with nothing.] [Verify: the declaration carries the threshold and refuses a value below 2 + exactly one write site for the counter, in the shared seam + the completion write and the reset are one update + a driven sequence of failures below the threshold opens no episode and one at the threshold opens exactly one + a streak that both opens and closes between two supervisor evaluations still opens its episode + the alert payload carries the observed run length + a corrupt counter value yields the unmeasurable verdict rather than a healthy one.]

A failure counter is not a retry policy and **MUST NOT** become one. The counter's only job is to make the failure rate visible to the supervisor; it does not decide whether to retry, skip, back off or disable the job. Where a project wants a circuit breaker, that is a separate decision with its own authority, and wiring it to this counter would give one number two meanings — the supervisor's threshold could then be moved to change runtime behaviour, which is [**SJO-N3**](scheduled-work-prohibitions-and-enforcement.md)'s widening with the consequences reversed.

Check reach. Two halves of this rule are mechanically decidable from a single file and one is not. Decidable: the declaration's presence and its minimum, which the registration signature's type plus a start-up refusal settle before any tick runs; and the single writer, which a rule keyed on the counter field's name appearing under a persistence update operator outside the seam's own file decides ([**FP-MS1**](../false-positive/mechanism-selection.md) — permission is the file's identity). Not decidable: whether the declared number is the right number. That is a product judgement over a failure-rate distribution that no source tree contains, which is why (e) exists — the measurement is carried in the alert so the number can be revised from data. A project that reads the presence of the rule as evidence that its thresholds are well-sized has made the **SJO-N2** inversion one level up.

## **MUST** (normative) — **SJO-M12**

**SJO-M12**: A recurring run that does not fit its cadence **MUST** be a supervised breach, measured on the same durable record as **SJO-M9** and judged by the same single health derivation. A run that takes as long as its cadence or longer completes, so liveness is satisfied and the failure streak resets; meanwhile the scheduler's next tick finds the mutual-exclusion lease held and is skipped. Each skip is a cadence slot whose work was never done, and no channel reports it. Four obligations. (a) The shared seam records each completed run's duration — acquisition to completion write — in the completion write itself, and compares it with the cadence interval already derived from the declaration; a second duration constant is a second answer to one question. (b) A tick refused because the lease is held by a run that started at an EARLIER tick and has not completed since is a lost slot and is counted; a tick lost to another instance of the same tick, or to a completed occurrence's hold (**SJO-M7**), is ordinary contention and is not. Telling them apart needs the holder's acquisition instant on the lease record, written by the acquisition write itself. (c) Over-cadence completions and lost slots feed ONE consecutive counter with a supervisor-consumed peak, atomically updated as in **SJO-M9**(d); a within-cadence completion resets the current streak but never the peak. The threshold is the declaration's consecutive-run threshold — how many consecutive bad slots a job tolerates is one product statement — and the episode, its single registered error and its consumption follow **SJO-M4**. (d) The alarm carries the measured streak, the threshold, the last run's duration and the cadence interval, so "how many slots were lost" and "how long the run takes" are read from the event, not re-derived from logs. Reason: a job that overruns is green on every other predicate while silently doing a fraction of its scheduled work. [Consequence: Ticks are lost in proportion to the overrun and the work the schedule promised quietly shrinks.] [Verify: driven runs — consecutive over-cadence completions and earlier-tick skips yield the overrun verdict at the threshold; same-tick contention, a held occurrence and within-cadence runs do not; a within-cadence run after an unsampled streak still leaves the peak for the supervisor. The counter's single writer is held by the same single-writer rule as **SJO-M9**'s counters.] A lease held in a store that keeps no durable acquisition instant cannot classify its skips; there the duration half alone holds the rule (**IV-M13**).
