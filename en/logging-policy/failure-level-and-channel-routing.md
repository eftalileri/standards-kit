## **MUST** — **M15** to **M19**

**M15**: Log a given failure once, at the outermost boundary that handles it (the single boundary handler — [error policy](../error-policy/error-model-registry-and-boundary.md) **M16**). The forbidden duplicate is an INTERSECTION, not a syntactic scope: an inner failure-level record (`warn` / `error`) plus a typed, registry-backed raise that is CERTAIN to reach a downstream boundary. Where the raise is certain, the boundary logs the same failure a second time — one request, two records, and the incident count stops meaning anything. `catch → log → rethrow` is the most familiar example of that intersection, not its defining condition: in one corpus-wide cleanup pass only three of twelve sites in this class sat inside a `catch`; the other nine were plain guard / rejection branches with no `catch` anywhere in scope (`if (condition) { log.warn(...); throw typedError }`). A rule or review that models `catch` alone reports "clean" while the class ships — when a check keeps missing a class, suspect the source model before its verdict (**M32**). Deleting the inner record is only half the fix: all of its structured meta (ids, `rootCause`, `solution`, driver evidence) **MUST** move into the raised carrier's context first. The raise at such a site is typically context-free, so the entire diagnosis lives inside the line being deleted; removing it without moving the meta is an information-loss regression, and the surviving boundary record carries only a bare code. A second, independent harm makes the inner record wrong even when its meta is duplicated elsewhere: an inner `error`-level call routes an expected (`warn`-category) failure into crash reporting, the exact inversion of **M17** / **N6**. Exception (unchanged): a standalone entry point with no outer boundary (CLI script, cron, job runner) logs at its own outermost catch (**N7**) — there the inner record is the *only* record and is legitimate. That exception is a layer/config decision (which paths sit behind a boundary), never a per-call-site judgment, and it is expressed by scoping the enforcement, not by softening it (**C8**).

Enforcement: the intersection is decidable from a single file's syntax tree — a failure-level log call followed, in the same statement list, by a typed raise. Where the adopting project uses source checks, this shape **MUST** be flagged by a static rule rather than left to review alone, without cross-file, registry, or aggregate analysis; other cases require review. Record the check's actual scope and limits. Four exclusions are deliberate; they are recorded here so the next reader learns the rule's boundary from the standard instead of reverse-engineering it from the rule source:

- `info` / `debug` / trace levels are out of scope. The boundary emits only `warn` and `error` (**M16**), so a record on the metric / trace channel (**M18**) cannot be a duplicate of it. Deliberately keeping a `debug` trail beside a removed `warn` is a legitimate pattern, not a finding.
- Log-and-swallow (log, then no raise) is a different class. It destroys evidence rather than duplicating it, and its remedy is the opposite one. Requiring a typed raise in the predicate keeps the two classes from being conflated under one message.
- Raw `throw` and bare rethrow are out of scope of THIS rule. They are not registry-backed carriers, the boundary handles them differently, and that shape is owned by the sibling raw-throw rule — one line must not be reported by two rules. The sibling **MUST** cover the rethrow shapes too, not only `throw new Error(...)`: a failure-level record followed by `throw <binding>` where the binding is the caught parameter (`catch (e) { log.error(..., e); throw e; }`) or a single constant constructed as a raw error is the same duplicate, and it is the worse one when the caught error is an expected registered code — the inner `error` record sends it to crash reporting. A transient provider outage (`OPERATIONAL`, retryable) wrapped this way opens its own crash-reporting issue and the job boundary records it again. The remedy is deletion of the inner record (remove a catch that only logs and rethrows) and, for precondition branches, a registered carrier with the meta. If a check temporarily uses a finding baseline, keep that baseline shrink-only and scoped to the exact finding shape.
- Conditional raises are out of scope. Where a branch, loop, or unrelated call separates the log from the raise, "the log's subject is the raised failure" is no longer provable from the tree. The rule stays on the narrow side rather than manufacturing false positives, since each one becomes a suppression line that later hides a genuine finding (**M32** (b) — widen only for measured, provable coverage).

**M15** process-boundary note: An uncaught exception or unhandled rejection has exactly one writer at any moment. Before the application logger exists (preloader to boundary registration) the crash-SDK preload module owns it: crash report at fatal level, value-scanned stderr text (**M25**), flush, non-zero exit. Once the process boundary registers, it retires that writer through an explicit handoff and becomes the only writer through the one logger seam (root cause from the carrier or the shared derivation, never a fixed "detail redacted" sentence; flush crash reporting and the logger, then a bounded exit). Every other process-level writer is a second record of the same crash and is forbidden: the crash SDK's default uncaught/unhandled integrations (they keep capturing after the boundary exists, without carrier context), the logging library's own exception/rejection handlers (an unscanned extra line, and they exit as soon as their transport finishes, racing the flush), and any feature-level process listener. Silencing the duplicate in the crash SDK's send hook is not the remedy (**M25** keeps that hook sanitize-only). A source-level check, where the adopting project uses one, may inspect listener placement, the logging library's own exception-handler configuration, SDK integrations and handoff order; SDK integration exclusion and the handoff call order otherwise remain a review obligation on the two boundary files. No single-file check can see that the preloader and the entry share one module instance at runtime; verify that with runtime evidence and document any loader-specific handoff requirement.

**M16**: Choose the level by error category ([error policy](../error-policy/error-policy.md)): `OPERATIONAL` / `BUSINESS` → `warn`; `SYSTEM` (and any unmapped code) → `error`. This is classification, not demotion — **M3** forbids hiding a `SYSTEM` incident at a lower level, but an expected error is `warn` by category, not a `SYSTEM` error pushed down to silence it.

**M17**: Only `error`-level (`SYSTEM`) failures may be forwarded to crash reporting (for example through a crash-reporting provider). Expected (`warn`) errors **MUST NOT** reach crash reporting (**N6**).

A record whose own text declares the failure expected, written at `error` level, is a self-contradiction and is the cheapest form of this violation to find. Degraded-mode call sites routinely label themselves — "degraded mode", "falling back to the authority", a documented tier name — and then log at the level that routes to crash reporting, so the text says *expected* while the level says *crash*. The harm is not that the incident is hidden but the inverse: the crash channel fills with events nobody will ever act on, and the genuine defects stop being visible inside that volume. The fix is two-part, and doing only the first half is a regression, not a partial improvement: demote the level to `warn` and carry the aggregate signal on the metric channel (**M18**). Demoting alone silences the crash channel and puts nothing in its place, so a flood of the expected failure — which is a real defect signal — becomes observable nowhere. Emit both from one writer so the log's searchable `action` tag and the counter's label cannot drift apart (**M18**, [**SSG-M8**](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)).

Enforcement: a crash-level logger call whose message is a static string carrying the adopting project's expected-degradation marker is decidable from one file, so a static check can flag this syntactic contradiction where such markers are used consistently. Stated limit: the check reads the call site's own text, not the path's semantics; an expected-degradation site that does not carry the marker stays outside the rule and remains a review obligation, and where the codebase has no stable marker convention the semantic classification is a review obligation. Severity stays decided at the single boundary.

**M17** corollary — a code defect detected at runtime without a throw (a repeated-read N+1, invariant drift, a contract mismatch) **MUST** be a registered `SYSTEM` code in the error registry, carried as an unthrown typed carrier through the single non-HTTP classification authority (the **M21** helper or its sibling next to it); it **MUST NOT** be a raw `error` call, a direct crash-SDK capture, or a level picked from the level table by the call site. A detector routes by what it proves: crossing a latency or count threshold is a symptom and stays `warn` + metric; a structural proof that round trips grow with the set ([**BHE-M13**](../backend-http-efficiency/set-loading-and-query-fan-out.md)) — the same value-free command shape repeated past its threshold inside one unit of work outside every declared legitimate loop invocation, or inside one declared invocation (a declaration exempts repetition across its invocations only) — is a defect. The proof is per command kind: a database read or write counts across the unit except the commands of a managed transaction's retry attempts, which the transaction runner marks and which are retry evidence also inside a declared invocation (a first attempt per item is a per-item repeat, never retry evidence); a cache command counts only as a sequential round trip (commands issued while the same shape is in flight are pipelined into one round trip); an outbound provider call counts per call; a separate unit started by the unit counts per start; a client calling the same route template for one actor on distinct items inside a short shared window counts as one client-driven repeat — an item is told apart by its resource path, by its value-free query and top-level body key sets, and by the values of item-identity keys only (calls that differ only in other values — search-as-you-type, paging, availability checks — or retry the same request are not per-item requests and are not counted). Each kind is its own registered code, and every defect record names the repeating code path (call sites captured only after the threshold, bounded per shape, declared commands included). Every definite shape of a unit is reported; a reporting cap applies to candidates only. A reached shape-tracking ceiling is a known blind spot and is a defect with its own registered code naming the unit, the first untracked shape and its call site. Transaction retry, pipelined fan-out and repetition across declared invocations stay `warn` + metric. One detection is one record: the defect record goes to crash reporting, and every occurrence is counted on the metric channel by the same authority, never by a second inline `warn` line. Volume ceiling: at most one crash event per defect identity per process per bounded window; the throttle is process-local because it is a noise ceiling, not shared state, and the metric still counts every occurrence. The window **MUST** be owned once by the defect channel (detectors pass a window length, never keep their own map or flag) and **MUST** open only after the provider accepts the event ([**M39**](crash-report-delivery-receipt.md) receipt); an event that is rejected or times out leaves no window, so the next occurrence retries. An occurrence inside the window (or while its event is in flight) is one `warn` line carrying the caller's context and the event id it repeats, excluded from the warn-signal window. Reason: a window consumed before delivery silently loses the defect for the whole window, and a suppressed occurrence with no line leaves no trace. Held by the channel's required window-length parameter (type) and review. The crash event carries a grouping fingerprint built from the registered code and the defect identity, valid at the event boundary by construction ([crash-report-delivery-receipt.md](crash-report-delivery-receipt.md)). A detector running in a response or event listener **MUST NOT** let any failure escape the listener (it would take the process down); its own failure is one `SYSTEM` record through the same non-HTTP authority, never swallowed. Reason: a runtime-proven defect is not an expected outcome, so it belongs in crash reporting; grouping and the ceiling keep one defect one issue instead of a flood.

**M18**: Aggregate signal for expected (`warn`) errors **MUST** be preserved on a separate metric channel (e.g. a counter keyed by code / category / status, exported for scraping) — independent of crash reporting. Silencing crash-report noise **MUST NOT** drop the aggregate signal; a flood of an expected error is still a defect signal and must remain observable somewhere. Nor may noise control SPLIT that signal. A searchable correlation label — a fixed action/event tag plus the companion field it is queried with — has exactly one writer ([**SSG-M8**](../ssot/ssot-principles-and-precedence.md)). The shape that violates this reads as defence in depth and is its opposite: a call site logs every occurrence inline while the shared authority is invoked only for the subset judged important, so the important occurrences are recorded twice and the routine ones only outside the authority — and a query filtering on the label returns a partial count with nothing indicating a remainder exists. Volume is a real concern with a correct home: the filter belongs inside the authority, or in the sink's retention/sampling configuration, never as a second inline writer. Widening the authority to record the routine occurrences too is a product decision whose cost — log volume rises — is stated where the authority is defined rather than discovered later from a bill. Where the adopting project uses static source checks, this label ownership can be enforced from a single syntax tree: the label's literal and its companion property key, anywhere outside the authority file. A volumetric bucket in front of the noisy path is not a volume filter, and the arithmetic says why. The reflex answer to an attacker-drivable flood of expected-failure lines is a rate limiter. Measure what the limiter's own rejection does first: where the over-limit response is produced by that same logging authority — which is the correct design, since the rejection is itself a registered expected failure — N requests still produce N lines, M of the original code and N−M of the limiter's, each carrying the same request snapshot. The bucket relabels the flood and changes neither its volume nor the caller-controlled content in it. What must be bound is the line, inside the authority, on a predicate the flood satisfies and legitimate traffic does not; prefer one derived from the request's own structure — *did any server route match this request* is such a predicate, and it exempts every defined endpoint by construction — over a list of paths or handler names, which ages into an allowlist. The budget is process-local on purpose: it is a noise ceiling, not shared state, so replicating it through a shared store would buy agreement nobody needs at the price of a new failure mode inside a boundary that must never throw.

A channel that exists but has no reader is not observability. The aggregate channel **MUST** have a reader that turns a sustained or sudden rise into exactly one alarm per episode through the system's existing episode-alarm authority; an exported counter nobody evaluates leaves the flood as invisible as no counter at all. Where scraping is not set up, the reader runs inside the existing supervised turn and judges a cross-instance window held in the shared store — a process-local counter cannot see the fleet total. Count every expected-failure line on exactly one aggregate counter: a line the expected-error boundary already counted is exempt from the generic warn-line counter only against proof of that count (a receipt the boundary's counter returns), never by a flag a call site can set. Lines written by the alarm machinery itself, and failures whose alarm another episode already owns, stay on the counter but are excluded from the reader's verdict, so one incident raises one episode. A request no server route matched **MUST** count its normalized method and its nearest served surface — the deepest registered route-template prefix, built from template segments only, never the client path — as its route dimension; a not-found rejection that no client URL builder can produce — no segment of its matched prefix is a route template end or router mount, so it stops at a bare path container such as the root or a shared `/api`-style namespace (an intermediate segment below a family root stays inside that family) or its raw path carries a `.`/`..` segment (raw or percent-encoded) — **MUST** stay on the counter and its line but be excluded from the verdict; the container test **MUST** read router mounts as well as template ends, or drift under a family prefix on which no template ends would read as container noise. Reason: an unmatched-route alarm without the requested surface cannot be acted on, and every client URL is a served template instance with dot-segments already removed, so such a flood is scanner noise rather than route-surface drift, which stays counted under every template or mount prefix; a first-segment-only test lets scanners that probe a shared namespace open drift episodes. Judge each label against its own baseline rather than a hand-kept list of "important" labels; select stricter thresholds by a structural predicate (for example provider- or server-side failure), never by enumeration. A code label is shared by every route that raises it, so one route rejecting nearly all of its requests drowns in that code's global baseline: the same reader **MUST** also judge each matched server route by its rejected share of completed requests in the window, against that route's own baseline share, from the same shared-store window. The numerator is marked by the expected-error boundary and counted with the denominator at one per-request writer, so both read the same route identity; a route-level baseline is read only for routes whose window already breaches, so the per-turn read does not grow with the route count. The route identity **MUST** be the full mounted template resolved from the matched route itself, never from mount state the framework rewinds when an error leaves a nested router, or rejections that reach the global error boundary pool under a prefix-less label. The route baseline **MUST** carry its own coverage: an hour before route counting existed is unmeasured, never "no requests", or every naturally rejecting route breaches after the first rollout. Expected failures a client produced itself enter the same window as server-counted fields under their own namespace, counted at the server's arrival minute (the client sends no time), together with closed loss fields for rejected and device-dropped occurrences; the same reader judges the namespace's total as ONE family subject with the same general rule — at most one episode for that unauthenticated family, carrying the top contributing fields — while server codes keep their per-code subjects ([**COB-RN-M6**](../client-observability-rn/classification-authority-and-outbound-links.md)). Reason: per-code volume answers "is this failure common?", never "is this flow broken?". A warn line without a valid fixed tag **MUST NOT** pool into one catch-all label; the counting authority derives its label from the line's source-literal message tag before falling back. Reason: a breach of a catch-all label cannot name the line that flooded, so the alarm is unactionable. Every counted occurrence **MUST** also count its source dimensions (route, producing unit, authenticated actor, tenant) in the same cross-instance window, length- and cardinality-capped, and every breach alarm **MUST** carry the top contributors per dimension in its evidence and rootCause. Reason: a count alone sends the operator to search logs; the event must name which route, unit and actor produced the flood. Contributor counts **MUST** be buffered under their own ceiling, never sharing the occurrence counts' capacity, and a dropped contributor **MUST NOT** be reported as a lost occurrence. Reason: during a store outage evidence fields outnumber occurrences and would evict the counts the verdict is computed from.

**M18** application — persisted-row defects seen on a read. A read that meets a defect stored in a row (a missing required reference, a stored copy disagreeing with its derivation, a snapshot that cannot be matched) **MUST** report it under a registered SYSTEM code once per row per bounded window, the "already reported" mark held in the shared cross-instance store; when that store cannot answer, the report **MUST** still be sent. It **MUST NOT** be logged on every read. Reason: the row stays broken between reads, so a per-read line floods the warn window with one defect while the crash channel never sees it. A designed retained state — a deleted subject's kept history, a raw reference whose populate missed — **MUST NOT** be reported as a defect. Reason: a permanent false event teaches operators to ignore the code. A defect the read path cannot prove (an ambiguous stored value) belongs to a declared read-only scheduled count that raises one event per run, never to a guess on the read.

**M18** application — an expected rejection that repeats one stored state. A rejection caused by the persisted state of one subject (a superseded, retired or deactivated session presented again) **MUST** count on the rate window once per subject per minute, while the metric counter and the log line still record every occurrence. Reason: one client's parallel in-flight requests otherwise read as a system-wide surge, while once per minute still lets a client that keeps retrying the dead state breach. The per-subject security judgement (distinct sessions of one account) reads the same first occurrence and **MUST NOT** lose it.

**M18** application — degradation that is not an error line. Route latency, outbound provider call outcome, cache miss share, queue oldest-pending age and dead-letter growth, and process memory and event-loop delay **MUST** be counted by their single existing seam (access summary, outbound call observer, cache read seam, backlog gauge setter, supervised round) into the same cross-instance window and judged by the same episode-alarm reader. Reason: a second detector or counter beside the window splits the signal and opens a second alarm path. A condition that is wrong in absolute terms (a route always slower than its budget, a queue stuck past its retry horizon) **MUST** be judged against an absolute threshold, not only against its own baseline. Reason: a baseline learns a permanent degradation as normal. A ratio whose normal level differs per member (a cache family that naturally misses) **MUST** also require departure from the member's own baseline share. A rate rule whose baseline is not yet established **MUST** still fire on its absolute floor. Reason: "unmeasurable" during the baseline's first day hides an outage that the floor alone already proves. A slow-request record **MUST** carry the wall time no data store accounts for and the share of it the process spent executing. Reason: without them "slow" cannot name database, cache, provider or the process itself as the root cause. A warn line describing a degradation its seam already counted (a slow request the route window holds) **MUST NOT** also count on the generic warn rate window; a record the seam did not count (an aborted or unmatched request) still does. Reason: the generic copy is a second latency alarm with a looser rule, and it fires on one client's start-up burst the dedicated judge deliberately ignores.

**M19**: The failure log meta **MUST** carry the same correlation / trace id the boundary returns to the client and that any crash-report event uses ([error policy](../error-policy/error-model-registry-and-boundary.md) **M17**), so log ↔ response ↔ crash report correlate. Include it in **M8**'s identifier set when present.
