## Global **MUST** / **MUST NOT** (this file)

| ID | Rule | Consequence | Verify |
|----|------|-------------|--------|
| **NOTIF-G1** | Every production notification sent through Expo tickets **MUST** implement the receipt path in [receipt-and-delivery.md](receipt-and-delivery.md). | Bug — **must not** ship; STOP until receipt handling is complete. | Review that the implementation distinguishes ticket acceptance from receipt outcome and records the lifecycle in the application’s notification operations documentation. |
| **NOTIF-G2** | User-visible or analytics “delivered/confirmed” **MUST** map only from `DELIVERED_CONFIRMED` (successful receipt), never from `SENT` alone. | Bug — false delivery UX or reporting. | Review state-to-copy and state-to-analytics mappings; ensure ticket acceptance alone never produces a delivered claim. |
| **NOTIF-G3** | A promotional or engagement push — one whose purpose is to nudge the recipient rather than to report something that just happened to them — **MUST** ask the project's single quiet-hours authority whether it may be delivered at this instant, in the recipient's canonical timezone, and **MUST NOT** re-implement that predicate locally. A scheduled send time that happens to fall outside quiet hours is not a substitute for the gate: the gate exists for the late attempt (a missed scheduler tick, a lease/backoff retry, a catch-up batch), which can move the same send into the recipient's night. When the gate closes, the channel defers to the authority's next permitted instant; it never downgrades to sending anyway. When the recipient's zone is missing or cannot be resolved, the authority **MUST** answer "quiet" with a logged reason (fail closed): an unknown local hour may be the recipient's night. A transactional push — a receipt, an earned reward, a state change the recipient caused or is entitled to know immediately — is exempt. The classification is declared, not inferred: a channel added or whose cadence is changed states its class where that cadence is defined, so the exemption is a written decision rather than an unexamined silence. An instant the recipient or the acting user chose themselves (a reminder time, a scheduled due moment) is honoured at that instant even inside quiet hours — the choice is theirs; only a catch-up attempt after that instant asks the authority, and a channel with no deferral store keeps a held recipient resumable rather than dropping it. Every send that does happen inside the recipient's quiet hours — a critical override or a chosen instant — **MUST** be counted on a fixed action after the provider accepted it, never at the decision, because evaluated candidates are not sends. This row ratchets forward — it binds new and edited channels, and does not demand a retroactive sweep of every existing sender. | Bug — a nudge wakes the recipient at night, or a night send happens and nobody can count it; or a second, divergent quiet-hours answer drifts from the first (SSG-M8). | Pass: for the channel under change, either its dispatch path consumes the single quiet-hours authority (grep the authority's exported symbol) or its cadence documentation states the transactional exemption; in neither case does the module define its own local-hour window comparison. |
| **NOTIF-G4** | A volume cap ("at most N pushes per period") binds only where its N has a single configured source. A channel outside that configuration **MUST NOT** have a cap invented for it, and **MUST NOT** be described as capped: either it consumes the same configured authority, or its cadence documentation states the structural bound that actually limits it (for example a durable natural key that admits one record per subject per period). Like NOTIF-G3 this row ratchets forward — it binds the channel under change. | Bug — a fabricated limit value becomes a second authority, or an uncapped channel is documented as capped and nobody enforces it. | Pass: the cap's N resolves to exactly one configuration authority (grep its consumers); the channel under change names, in its own cadence documentation, either that authority or the structural bound it relies on instead. |
| **NOTIF-G5** | When several candidate notifications compete for one delivery slot of the same recipient, each candidate's own eligibility — its repeat limit and its cooldown — **MUST** be decided before the slot is assigned, through the same decision function the final send check uses; a candidate that cannot be delivered **MUST NOT** win the slot. A candidate whose occurrence already holds a durable decision (sent, delivered on another channel, cancelled, or in flight) is likewise removed before selection, from the occurrence key's durable state read without an intent comparison (no channel is chosen yet). Recipient-wide caps that bind every candidate equally (daily, weekly, silence) may be checked after selection; such a block is not a decision about the occurrence and **MUST NOT** claim its durable key (IDEMP-M7: a mutable-quota rejection releases). | Bug — a candidate whose own limit is exhausted keeps winning every run, is cancelled at send time, and the recipient receives no other notification; or the selection-time and send-time eligibility copies drift apart; or a decided or temporarily blocked occurrence collides with its own row on every run and is never delivered after the block lifts. | Pass: the selection path and the final send check call the same eligibility function (grep its consumers); the selection site re-derives no repeat or cooldown predicate of its own; a blocked send writes no row under the occurrence key. |
| **NOTIF-G6** | A recipient-wide volume cap counts only the notification class it bounds. A cap on automatic (rule-driven engagement) notifications **MUST** count only that class's successful deliveries, read from a store or filter that holds no other class; a transactional notification (a report of something that happened to the recipient — a confirmation, a reminder of their scheduled item, a payment) **MUST NOT** consume that budget and **MUST NOT** be blocked by it. | Bug — the most active recipients, who receive transactional pushes every day, are silently excluded from every automatic notification, and the cap reports a block nobody can trace to an automatic send. | Pass: the cap's counter query selects only the capped class (grep the reader it calls); no transactional sender reads or writes that counter. |
| **NOTIF-G7** | A goal-driven engagement sequence — several outbound steps on any channel (push, in-app, a billed message) that exist to make the recipient perform one action — **MUST** (a) re-read the goal fact from its domain authority immediately before each step's provider call and end the sequence at the first goal fact, never relying on a selection made earlier in the run; (b) send each step on exactly one channel, chosen by one channel-selection function, so a step that falls back moves to the next channel instead of adding one; (c) measure its success only from the goal's domain facts, never from its own send, delivery or tap records, which may only be reported as reach. | Bug — a recipient who already acted is nudged (and on a billed channel, paid for), one step reaches the same person twice, or the sequence reports its own deliveries as success and nobody can tell whether it changed behaviour. | Review: the send step calls the goal reader after selection and before the provider call; one channel choice per step key; the success read-out's query touches no notification or outbox store. |
| **NOTIF-G8** | An engagement nudge (NOTIF-G3 class) about a tenant or account on a time-limited trial **MUST** ask one window authority whether that tenant is still inside its engagement window. The window **MUST** end with the trial's last local day, counted from the trial start's local day as day 1 with the adopting project's single trial-length value; extending the trial does not extend it. A paying tenant is outside the window rule; a tenant neither paying nor on trial gets no nudge. A sequence whose steps may run on channels of different cost **MUST** take its step days and its lifetime cap from the channel the recipient is reached on at evaluation, so the costlier channel keeps its sparser plan. | Bug — a new owner keeps getting generic engagement nudges after the trial they were offered, each channel counts its own trial day and they drift, or a billed channel sends at the free channel's cadence. | Review: every engagement channel's eligibility calls the one window function (grep its consumers); no channel compares a trial day or trial length of its own; the sequence's day table and cap are keyed by channel. |
| **NOTIF-N1** | Must not treat `ticket.status` `ok` alone as proof of FCM / APNs delivery to the device (see NOTIF-RCP-M1). | Bug — **must not** ship. | Pass: UI / API **must not** expose “delivered” for `SENT` only — grep `DELIVERED_CONFIRMED` / `SENT` in notification code paths per NOTIF-RCP-M2 Verify. |

NOTIF-G1 verification: Confirm the application documents the ticket-to-receipt lifecycle and that its implementation follows the receipt state rules.

NOTIF-G3 / NOTIF-G4 verification boundary: Whether a channel is promotional or transactional, and what volume limit applies to it, are semantic product decisions. Record the classification and chosen authority for each changed channel. Review that dispatch paths use that authority and do not implement their own local quiet-hours or cap calculation. These decisions are not provable from a single call site, and a rule that merely checks for importing the dispatcher would also fire on every exempt transactional sender.

NOTIF-G8 verification boundary: Whether a send is an engagement nudge is the same semantic class NOTIF-G3 cannot read from syntax; review holds it.
