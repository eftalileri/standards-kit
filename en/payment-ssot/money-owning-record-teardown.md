## MUST (normative) — PAY-M

| ID | Rule | Consequence | Verify |
|----|------|-------------|--------|
| **PAY-M10** | Collection ledgers are retained history. Every delete **MUST** consult one disposability authority, returning false for any payment line or linked usage/history record—even offsetting lines whose sum is zero. Count entries, not only their net sum. Ad-hoc "was anything paid" copies are forbidden. | Irreversible financial/history loss — must not ship | Inspect every model delete and teardown caller: one disposability gate or explicitly chosen money scope under PAY-M11. |
| **PAY-M11** | Relationship changes (membership, assignment, transfer or cycle close) **MUST NOT** destroy money records by default. Destruction is separately named and explicitly chosen before execution, with concrete consequence, known attributable revenue removed and irreversibility. Unknown/unattributable amount lines are omitted, never approximated; confirmation promises **MUST** match actual retention. | Irreversible financial/history loss — must not ship | Compare choice and confirmation line by line with retained/deleted write results. |
| **PAY-M12** | Close and delete **MUST** be separately named authorities, never preserve/delete branches behind one function's flag. Close never deletes the money-owning record; the delete function states destruction in its name. Existing API scope parameters may dispatch to these authorities but never pass a flag into a shared destructive tail. | Irreversible financial/history loss — must not ship | Inspect distinct function names and absence of preserve/delete flags inside either authority. |
| **PAY-M13** | Owner-link unsetting or nulling **MUST NOT** substitute for deletion. Only cascade (target plus referencing rows) or preservation (keep target and valid references, stop future use) is permitted; sever-then-delete creates irrecoverable orphan history. Existing orphans are repair data, never precedent. | Irreversible financial/history loss — must not ship | Inspect owner-reference writes with the adopting project's enforcement mechanism (recording its actual scope and limits); inspect owner-snapshot rows for missing owner links. |
| **PAY-M21** | A teardown **MUST** classify every owner-keyed collection as deleted, retained, de-identified or re-homed; absence is omission. Classification lives at the authority, one entry per collection, with reasons for retained/de-identified rows. Deletes use each collection's single authority. User-created children of a system-created successor **MUST** be re-homed to the restored record through their bind authority before deletion in the same unit; stored system-writer provenance decides, erring toward re-home rather than erase. Unclassified orphans can inflate aggregates or block legitimate unique keys. Legacy repair follows [REP-M-23](../repository/repository-irreversible-operation-evidence.md) only after the producing omission is fixed. | Irreversible history loss, orphan inflation or blocked legitimate identity | Review the schema and classify every owner-keyed collection; where the adopting project has a coverage check that discovers owner-key/reference schema paths and verifies direct or actually-called delegated delete bodies, it proves declaration and written deletion, not retention judgement. Composite or unfamiliar owner keys require manual declaration; class correctness remains review. |
| **PAY-M19** | The "never delete directly" list is an irreversibility list, not a money list — and its exemptions are per model. A record the law requires to be de-identified rather than deleted (identifying fields unset, row retained) sits on the ledger row's fence. Two consequences. (a) One shared exemption list across models is a silent hole: a path authorised to tear down model A inherits authority over model B the moment B joins the list, the exemption living far from its reason. Exemptions are declared per model; a model whose only legitimate write is de-identification declares an empty one. (b) The message must not assert one model's reason for all — a wrong reason hides the real one, which belongs at the registration site. | Irreversible loss of a retained-by-obligation record, or an exemption that widened unowned | Per-model exemption map in the check's config, empty for de-identify-only models; inspect a cross-model delete against the per-model map; another model's exemption must not authorize it. |
| **PAY-M26** | A confirmed history-bearing child erasure **MUST** bind consent to its preview, unwind dependencies atomically and retain settled lines ([clauses](#pay-m26-clauses)). | Unseen money silently erased, a broken balance equation that fails every later read, or a settlement line pointing at a record that no longer exists — must not ship | Inspect the input, write and read paths against the clauses below. |

A relationship exit whose declared contract ends future continuation must close that intent through its canonical writer on every retained record in the exit scope, regardless of its activity flag. Inactivity is not evidence that a continuation intent ended. Preserve money and usage history, and keep the existing disposability authority separate; the scoped selection, intent closure and relationship detachment share one transaction snapshot. The single intent writer is the stronger mechanism, so no further check is admitted.

Child history — [**PAY-M10/M11/M26**](#must-normative--pay-m) clauses. Revenue-accruing or entitlement-consuming children and their payment/refund/accrual rows remain history even with net zero or unpaid payout. The single disposability decision reads those ledgers and status economics in the deleting unit. Empty pending/cancelled rows may be deleted; history refuses with its registered business error without PAY-M26 consent.

Single-child deletion and an explicit full parent-bundle purge offer child-history consent. A parent preview combines its ledger and each child's history through the same authorities, binding exact child IDs. Series closure, renewal teardown, relationship removal and assignee maintenance keep plain refusal. Deletion of the owning subject **MUST NOT** refuse on, or delete, a history-bearing pending child: in the deleting unit, before any child is disposed, the single disposability decision selects it and the status-transition authority moves it to the status whose PAY-D11 row expects no payment, ledgers untouched. No refund, paid cancellation, accrual or receivable is written — each is a separate user choice (PAY-M11, PAY-M24) — and the retained child can no longer be worked as live or promoted to revenue by an expiry job. The deletion's confirmation states this and that a refund is issued before the deletion. Bulk/parent paths gate every candidate before cascade writes, then delete exactly checked IDs plus original tenant predicates; new matching rows cannot enter unchecked. Without consent the parent ledger passes disposability and independent child history remains protected; with consent settled children retain their lines under (d), once per unit behind one closure fence. A single-writer check may confine direct child deletion to the registered persistence seams; caller decision, projection and snapshot agreement remain review-held.

Complete tenant erasure is a separately chosen money scope under PAY-M11. It **MUST** delete the tenant-owned record/bundle/payment/accrual graph through each existing authority, including settlement ledgers and their retained twins, when the product decision preserves none of that graph. This exception **MUST NOT** extend to a partial child erasure while an immutable settlement still references its lines. Classify every collection under PAY-M21 and use the durable closing/resume authority for a graph spanning transactions; a product retention decision is not statutory evidence.

Unused-record exception — [**PAY-M11**](#must-normative--pay-m). A relationship operation may erase a record and its never-collected open receivable only when, after the operation, no linked usage/history or collected-payment line remains. The same disposability authority decides, never a caller copy. Confirmation **MUST** state this condition and preservation of every other open receivable, omitting any amount that cannot be attributed to the retained/erased scope. Copy-to-write agreement is reviewed line by line.

Non-owning parent deletion — [**PAY-M11/M13**](#must-normative--pay-m) clauses. PAY-M13's owner reference is the field that says who OWNS the rows (subject, tenant); an optional relationship pointer to an organising parent (a group, a category) is not one, and deleting such a parent is neither a cascade over money-bearing children nor a preserve of the parent. The children and their history stay whole and the delete runs as ONE atomic unit: (1) the unit first writes the parent's shared technical fence, so a racing child writer conflicts and retries against the fresh state; (2) every relationship pointer is cleared through the pointer's single writer, on children of EVERY state, found from the referencing side — never from the parent's own member list, which can lag the pointers; (3) every LIVE link that lets a screen open or act on the parent (the pointer and any shared session identity derived from it) is cleared on every dependent row through the link's single detacher, and the denormalised label that names the parent is cleared with it wherever the row is still active or future; on a row whose lifecycle is already settled (completed, cancelled) the label MAY stay as read-only history, because it opens nothing and erasing it would silently delete what the user already sees as their past — the label's consumers must render it as history only (never re-derive a live parent from it), and the detacher takes the label's fate as a required parameter so no caller inherits a silent default; (4) only then is the parent deleted. Nothing else on a child is deleted, cancelled or rewritten (recurrence intent, plan, history, collections and receivables stay), so a recurring child simply continues as an independent record under its own authority. Repair of rows orphaned before the unit existed is a separate, separately approved production step with a dry run (REP-M-23, REP-M-25).

Receivable choice on close — [**PAY-M11**](#must-normative--pay-m) clauses. A relationship or schedule-closing operation that KEEPS the money-owning record (clearing a bundle's future, removing a member from a group, deleting a series) keeps its collected lines by construction; it must equally not decide the fate of the record's open (unpaid) receivable as a side effect — cancelling that claim is a money write, not a relationship change, and an automatic cancellation behind copy that says "the record is kept" is the silent destruction PAY-M11 forbids. Rules: (a) the default of every such operation is that the open receivable stays; (b) where the operation has a user-facing confirmation, the cancellation is a separately named choice offered next to the concrete consequence, the remaining unpaid amount when the actor may see it, and an irreversibility statement, and it travels as an explicit request field — the closed value set `keep | cancel` — never inferred; (c) the amount is the registered receivable derivation's output, never re-derived at the call site or on the client; (d) visibility is part of consent (PAY-M26 (b)): an actor who may not cancel a receivable, or may not see its amount, is not offered the cancellation — they still complete the operation, with the receivable kept, so a money-visibility restriction limits the money write and never blocks the workflow; (e) an API-only entry point without a confirmation surface keeps the receivable, never cancels it; (f) the cancellation itself goes through the receivable-cancellation writer and its write-authority decision, and a money write refused after tenant closure began is refused before the first write. Released-client compatibility (time-bounded, [DCD-M8](../dead-code/dead-code-rules.md)): a client that predates the field sends none; the server keeps that client's earlier behaviour until the oldest such release leaves the field, then deletes the branch in one change.

Enforcement limit while the full delete graph is closed: one file's syntax tree can detect a direct delete lacking a same-function gate call, but it cannot prove the candidate projection contains every ledger field, that the gate dominates the delete on all branches, or that read and write share one transaction session. Those facts require the cross-file data-flow and unit review at every caller; a green check result is never proof of them.

## Confirmed erasure clauses — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses)

A user-confirmed erasure of a history-bearing child record **MUST** be bound to the summary the user was shown, **MUST** unwind every dependent record through that record's own authority in one atomic unit, and **MUST** retain — never delete, never rewrite — any line that an immutable settlement ledger references. Where the product decides that a child record carrying money or usage history (payment, refund or accrual lines, a realized visit) may be deleted on the user's explicit choice — the separately named destructive option of PAY-M11 — the permission changes *whether* the delete runs, never *how consistently* it runs. Five obligations bind.

<a id="pay-m26-a-preview-then-a-bound-confirmation"></a>

<a id="preview-and-bound-confirmation-pay-m26a"></a>

### Preview and bound confirmation — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses) (a)

One server-side summary of what the erasure removes (line counts per ledger, the amounts the actor may see, the realized-visit flag) is derived from the same single disposability decision the plain delete uses (PAY-M10); the confirmation the client sends back is an opaque token derived from that summary, and the erasing unit recomputes the summary on its own fresh transactional read and refuses with one registered conflict code when the token differs — a line the user was not shown is never erased. A request without a token keeps the plain registered refusal, so a released client that cannot show the preview stays safe and sees a sensible message.

<a id="pay-m26-b-visibility-is-part-of-consent"></a>

<a id="visibility-and-consent-pay-m26b"></a>

### Visibility and consent — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses) (b)

Visibility decides what the preview shows, never whether the erasure may be confirmed (money visibility limits what is seen, never the workflow). For an actor who may not see the summary's money (the preview summary is an aggregated figure, SEC-N-RESP-6) the preview omits the whole summary — line counts as well as amounts, because the count per line type discloses a payment status the read boundary hides — rather than approximating it (PAY-M11 (b)); the warning states only that records the actor cannot see are erased with it and that settled lines stay (d). The confirmation token still binds the full server summary, so a line added after the preview is never erased (a). Any token or digest that reaches an actor who may not see the summary **MUST** be a server-keyed MAC, never a plain hash of the summary: the summary's value space (a few line counts, amounts near known catalogue prices) is small enough to search offline. A hidden-summary preview **MUST** always carry its consent material; one without it is a contract breach rejected at the response boundary, never rendered as a dead end.

<a id="pay-m26-c-owned-ledgers-go-with-their-owner-derived-counts-are-recomputed"></a>

<a id="owned-ledgers-and-derived-counts-pay-m26c"></a>

### Owned ledgers and derived counts — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses) (c)

Ledger lines embedded in and owned only by the child are deleted with it (PAY-M13 (a)); every derived figure that counted the child (entitlement use, balances, reports) is recomputed by its single derivation in the same unit or derived at read time — never patched by a second writer.

<a id="pay-m26-d-settled-lines-are-retained-not-orphaned"></a>

<a id="settled-line-retention-pay-m26d"></a>

### Settled-line retention — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses) (d)

A line that an immutable settlement ledger elsewhere references (for example an accrual that has already been paid out) moves into a retained twin record keyed by the settlement's own reference and read wherever live lines are read — the balance reader and every itemised report alike, or the summary and its line list disagree; the fields an itemised reader enriches from the erased child are frozen onto the twin at retention through the same enrichment helpers, because afterwards no source can derive them; the settlement ledger is not edited and its balance equation (accrued = paid + outstanding) keeps holding. The retained class is declared at the owner-teardown authority (PAY-M21).

<a id="pay-m26-e-a-child-that-closed-a-parents-entitlement-period-is-a-choice-not-a-refusal"></a>

<a id="closed-period-child-choice-pay-m26e"></a>

### Closed-period child choice — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses) (e)

Deleting a used child of a closed entitlement period **MUST** offer separately named server-derived outcomes and numbers, binding the selected outcome and parent closure/entitlement/usage facts into consent:

- Return the session: conditionally clear closure and derive used/remaining. Proven already-issued renewal stays; end the reopened parent's renewal intent through its writer to prevent a second successor. Without surviving renewal evidence, preserve user intent so it renews normally again; preview distinguishes these cases. Evidence includes pre-link renewals of the same subject/offering born after closure with no link.
- Keep closed: reduce working entitlement by the erased count through its closed writer set, leaving remaining and closure unchanged. Below-contract-minimum results are unavailable and raise the registered refusal; the unchanged remaining makes PAY-R-M9(f) structurally satisfied without another gate.

Both require parent-write authorization, not merely child ownership. Other actors see a reasoned closed-parent notice without choices; a supplied outcome raises the registered forbidden code on the unit's fresh read before writes. Missing choice keeps plain refusal for released clients.

Bulk selection offers one outcome for all eligible children, never skips them; "keep closed" requires every parent's entitlement minus selected count to meet minimum. Same-parent siblings run sequentially. Each follow-up re-reads preview immediately before erase and requires unchanged displayed history — for a hidden summary, an unchanged server-keyed history-only digest, since the token also binds parent facts the earlier erasure moved; parent facts moved by earlier erasures are freshly read, not compared as stale. If its chosen outcome is no longer offered, do not erase that child.

<a id="pay-m26-verification-and-check-boundaries"></a>

<a id="verification-and-check-boundaries-pay-m26"></a>

### Verification and check boundaries — [**PAY-M26**](#pay-m26-clauses)

Inspect token comparison after the fresh read and before the first write; same-session retention before deletion; all balance/itemised readers unioning retained lines; and closed-parent outcomes preserving closure/count consistency. Those domination, session and reader-set facts are cross-file, and single deletion and explicit parent purge share one decision, so a single-file check cannot cover them. Where the adopting project has a retained-twin classification check or a tenant-closure fence check, record its actual scope and limits.
