## **MUST**

**REP-M-18**: **Introducing a new immutable completion/outcome discriminator into existing durable data **MUST NOT** infer that outcome from a generic legacy status such as `completed`.** Adopt historical records only through an explicit-mode migration whose read-only preflight proves the required identity, owner/scope, source or environment, ordering evidence, canonical payload binding, and source snapshot independently of the missing discriminator. Require only the evidence applicable to the domain, but document its authority. A mutable current projection is not historical proof unless its source-owned ordering value and identity match the legacy event and are frozen in the apply snapshot. Conversely, an event with its own immutable completion evidence is authoritative and **MUST NOT** be rejected solely because a later current projection has changed.

Malformed, contradictory, or unprovable identity-bearing records **MUST** be counted by reason and block all writes until an authorized resolution supplies evidence; do not delete, merge, reassign, or guess. Apply against the frozen source snapshot with an exact compare-and-set and perform a final read-back.

When live writers must change in coordination with the migration, use a backward-compatible staged cutover. First deploy readers/writers that tolerate both old and new records and establish a durable coordination fence for every mutation that can change the identity evidence. Wait until every active writer participates and pre-existing transactions have drained. Then quiesce or otherwise fence new claims while applying and verifying the migration. New events arriving during the transition must be durably retained for later processing without spending retry budget or performing effects against ambiguous state. After data, constraints, and versioned readiness markers pass a fresh postflight, deploy the new writer while the fence remains closed; release it only by a compare-and-set against the same generation and verified current state. Boot and writer boundaries fail closed if exact topology, marker versions, or migrated-data invariants are missing or malformed. Keep unbounded inventory and drain scans in an explicit migration/operator path, not normal boot. A marker is written only after its final postflight; a model declaration is not a substitute for live evidence. Use database serialization rather than cross-host application timestamps to order concurrent writes. [Consequence: A legacy no-op is misclassified as a business effect, historical identity is assigned to the wrong owner, or live traffic consumes retries or performs effects during an unsafe cutover.] [Verify: Inventory legacy records; dry-run/apply/read-back evidence from independent sources; frozen-source CAS; compatible-reader rollout and durable-writer fence; transaction drain; deferred-event handling; unresolved-record resolution; exact topology and readiness markers; fail-closed boot and writer behavior. This spans historical data and concurrent writers, so it cannot be established by a single-file check.]

**REP-M-20**: A field's absence in a stored record is evidence about that record's age, not by itself evidence that its writer is broken. A derivation newly bound to a persisted field does not reach records written before that field existed. Before recording a missing-field finding, compare the field's introduction in the schema and writer with the record's own write time, and state both facts. A record predating the field cannot contain it; reading that absence as a current writer defect misdirects remediation. The converse — treating a missing outcome discriminator as proof that an outcome happened — is forbidden by **REP-M-18**.

When a fail-loud derivation newly depends on a persisted field, name the path that brings existing records into compliance: an explicit backfill, or a specific write path and the event that triggers it. State the reachable population and the worst-case latency. “Eventually” without a trigger is not a migration plan. A preflight **MUST NOT** require a field written only by a later stage of its own sequence; establish dependency direction first. Report named, counted classes rather than one unresolved total, including out-of-scope records, records explained by age, and genuinely unresolved records by reason. Any excluded class **MUST** be declared using the contract's published field, not a process-local setting that changes with the operator's environment. A backfill **MUST NOT** treat a denormalized stamp as proof of the relation when another writer also produced such stamps: it requires independent provenance and consistency with the owning authority, and counts every other row as unwritten.

Retention-clock rename application: If a database-native expiry mechanism is deleting the wrong class of records, a replacement clock field may provide a safer staged transition. Use a distinct field name and stop writing the superseded field before removing its expiry structure, so protected records become unreachable to the old expiry mechanism before DDL cleanup. Declare the reason and end condition where the new field is written; do not include a superseded structure in the target topology after it is removed. Before cleanup, verify the replacement structure and measure whether protected records lack the new clock. This pattern applies only where the datastore's expiry semantics make records without the indexed field ineligible; verify that behavior for the selected database.

Absent record versus transition state: Where a new field models a transition, distinguish a record with a non-passing or contradictory state from no transition record at all. The former may represent in-progress or unknown work and remains fenced. The latter may mean the transition never began and must follow the pre-field behavior, observably. Represent absence separately when the type system supports a discriminated state; an optional field alone can hide the distinction. [Consequence: Correct historical records are treated as broken, valid old records are rejected by a new derivation, or absence is confused with an in-progress transition.] [Verify: Findings pair field introduction with record age; every new persisted dependency names its backfill/reach path, trigger, and latency; exclusions are named and counted from authoritative fields; transition consumers handle absent record separately from non-terminal state. History and production data are required evidence, so a single-file check cannot decide these conditions.]
